Skills I built

AI agent skills for small and mid-sized businesses

Each skill is a complete workflow an AI agent runs from start to finish: it gathers the inputs, works through each step, checks quality and delivers a finished report or action.

01

Amazon New Product Discovery

Amazon sellers keep facing the same question: what should we launch next? Starting from the seller's own ASINs or a storefront, this skill maps the competitive landscape. It finds direct competitors and the competitors behind them, catalogs their storefronts, and spots gaps in product types, pack sizes, bundles, price tiers and variants. It then mines reviews for unmet needs, scans adjacent products, checks each opportunity against real demand signals, and filters for brand fit. The output is an opportunity report (Google Doc) and a competitive landscape workbook (Google Sheets), linked by opportunity number. Large catalogs are split into parallel research batches. The skill lays out the evidence and leaves the final call to the seller.

02

Social Media Marketing Agent

Small brands know they should post consistently, but few have time to research what works and keep up across five platforms. This marketing copilot has two modes. Research scans competitors in the niche and delivers a chat summary, a Google Doc report and a screenshot album. Create learns the brand's voice and brand kit, then turns one idea into drafts written for each platform (LinkedIn, X, TikTok, Instagram and Facebook) and shows them on an approval card where the owner can edit them. It is designed to schedule and publish through a virtual browser using the owner's own logged-in accounts, so it can do what the owner can do on each platform with no API keys. It prefers each platform's own scheduler, has a self-healing layer that adapts when a platform changes its layout, confirms posts actually went live, and never publishes without the owner's sign-off.

03

Ads Audit

Small businesses often spend on ads across several platforms with no clear view of what's working. This skill audits paid media across Google, Meta, Amazon, LinkedIn, TikTok, Microsoft and other channels from whatever the owner uploads (exports, screenshots, or links). Specialist agents review tracking, budget, creative, compliance and each platform in parallel. The results roll up into a health score and grade, quick wins, and a 7/30/90 day action plan, delivered as a Google Doc report plus scorecard sheets. An optional full audit adds competitor research, and returning clients are remembered so repeat audits start faster.

04

SEO & GEO Audit

Customers now find businesses through AI answers like ChatGPT and Google AI as well as through Google search. This skill audits both. It checks Google Maps to see whether the business has a Google Business Profile, then follows one of two audit paths: one for local businesses with a profile, one for online-only brands. It crawls the site and sitemap, runs keyword searches, pulls Google Business Profile data, and checks technical SEO, schema, and Shopify, WordPress and Wix specifics. Google Search Console, Analytics, Ahrefs or SEMrush can be connected for deeper data. Every section of the report spells out the issues found, what to do, and the GEO impact. The report runs through a QA checklist before it's delivered as a Google Doc.

05

Email Marketing Manager Pro

SMB outreach usually fails one of two ways: spam filters or a burned sender reputation. This skill runs multi-touch email campaigns from a connected Gmail or Outlook account the way a careful person would. It imports contact lists from files or shared links, drafts each touch for the owner's approval, sends one email at a time at a human pace during business hours, and follows up only with verified non-responders. It tracks replies, bounces and unsubscribes in one contact status view, honors opt-outs permanently, adds an unsubscribe and address footer to every email, and drafts replies to warm leads for approval.

06

CRM Pipeline Manager

Many small teams can't justify a heavy CRM, so deals live in inboxes and spreadsheets. This is an AI-native CRM the team runs in plain language: accounts, contacts, deals, tasks, activity history and pipeline forecasting. A guided setup interview captures the team's real sales process, and data can be migrated from Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive or spreadsheets. Permission tiers decide what the agent does on its own versus what needs approval, every change is logged and can be undone, and daily digests and reports flag deal risk and next best actions.

07

Customer Service Email Automation

Small businesses lose customers when support emails sit unanswered, but most owners aren't ready to let AI reply on their behalf. This skill learns the business from its website, policy pages, shared files and past customer emails, then drafts replies in the owner's own voice. Every draft goes to an approval queue where the owner can approve, edit or reject it, and every edit feeds back into how it learns. It grounds answers only in the business's real policies and live order or booking data, escalates sensitive issues like legal threats or chargebacks straight to a person, flags emails still waiting on a reply, and logs questions the website couldn't answer. Once a category of email has earned a strong approval record, the agent can propose auto-responding to that category only, with ongoing spot checks. The owner can take that autonomy back instantly.

08

Due Diligence

Before a partnership, acquisition, investment or big sales pitch, small teams rarely have the time or analysts for deep company research. From just a company name or URL, this skill produces a full due diligence report covering the company profile and business model, market sizing, competitive positioning, digital presence, customer reputation, product and IP, financial signals, leadership and growth indicators, then a SWOT and strategic considerations. Three research agents work in parallel to keep it fast, and each section passes a quality check before the report is assembled. Where public evidence is thin, the report says so instead of filling the gap with guesses. It's delivered as a Google Doc, a OneDrive document, or a PDF, depending on what the owner has connected.
